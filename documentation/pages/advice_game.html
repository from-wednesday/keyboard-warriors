Keyboard Warriors · Playing the Game

"No one reads the manual"

That's what they say and yet here you are! Good for you.


This isn't a complete list of features. I'm not going to explain everything. The interface is intentionally playful to promote exploration. It's not up front with all it can do. Instead, the app starts simple and only exposes new features as you hit milestones to reward curiosity and practice. I hope to surprise and delight those that stick with their training over time.


For those wanting a head start on that journey, here is how to get the most out of a few core concepts:

Physical and Virtual Keyboards

On touch devices without a connected keyboard, the onscreen keyboard is active by default.

For all other users (that is to say, most) the graphical keyboard is not active by default.

Why? Partly to encourage you to use your physical keyboard. That is the best way to build your muscle memory with the shortcuts.

But that's not the only reason. Some shortcuts are used by your browser, operating system, or extensions, intercepting them before the game can detect they were pressed.

Keyboard Warriors has several systems to actively manage these issues:

  • It avoids asking you to press keys that we can't read on your system.
  • If any do slip through, the system can detect, mitigate and report them.

The onscreen keyboard sidesteps these issues entirely. Because the game knows exactly which virtual keys are being pressed, there is no need to skip conflicted shortcuts.

For this reason, shortcut conflict management is paused whenever the onscreen keyboard is active (except for missing extended keyboard keys).

This brings us to an interesting scenario. What if you do have a keyboard and also turn on the virtual keys?

Now you are free to be asked conflicted questions and there are no guard rails to stop you pressing those keys on your actual keyboard.

So in this situation you will see the keys that we expect to cause a problem light yellow.

Once this is understood, many players find that using both keyboard and touch offers the widest range of training opportunities. It surfaces the full vocabulary of questions. For most you can build your skill with the real keyboard. When a conflicted shortcut does come up, you will know to use the on screen keyboard to complete that chord.

Additional notes:

  • If the system detects a blocked key or chord during play, it will silently unblock it for you.
  • If in the course of play another shortcut interrupts the game, you'll be prompted to confirm the conflict and optionally report it.

Keyboard Display Modes

Other controls in the keyboard settings panel give you control over whether and how the keyboard is shown on screen. The Graphical Keyboard display is on by default.

On small phone screens it will be out of view, however the screen will auto scroll down and up with the cadence of question and answer.

The default experience on most screens is to have the keyboard visible but inactive. The intention, as mentioned earlier, is that you practice with your actual keyboard.

But having the graphical keyboard on screen helps you learn shortcuts and convert them to memory:

  • The success/fail key lighting assists you in learning unfamiliar shortcuts.
  • The visual patterns of chord shapes imprint on your memory.

Turning keyboard visibility off makes the game harder, suiting advanced learners looking for a greater challenge. It forces you to rely upon your internal mental model of where keys actually are.

But there is also an interim setting. Ghost Mode turns the keyboard invisible providing the challenge of relying on recall, not recognition, but in Ghost Mode the lighting still functions. So you still receive the two benefits listed for the default visible keyboard! I think this setting might be the ideal one for confident players.

Debrief with 'Report Card' + 'History View'

After completing a round you will land on the Report Card. You get some shortcuts from the previous round highlighted showing what the coach believes you have learnt, and which there is room to improve.

There will usually be some advice customised to how you are tracking.

If the coach recommends settings changes there will be a button to instantly apply the recommendation.

You can also toggle between this screen and a History View that provides more detailed statistics on the entire round of questions.

In both the report card and the history view (as well as the in-game cheat sheet if you have found it) players with a connected keyboard can rehearse the listed shortcuts and they will light accordingly.

Freestyle Discovery

When between rounds, if all settings screens are closed (that includes the end of round recap) the keyboard will be facing you idle. Keys pressed at this time are matched against the entire library of keys and chords in the data file for the currently selected app.

Anything pressed that will do something in the currently selected app will be reported in the heading area. This provides a unique way to explore an application's shortcut vocabulary through direct experimentation.

Notes on freestyle discovery:

  • Sometimes the same key or keys can do more than one thing based on context. The freestyle system only reports the first hit it finds, so I am prioritising core functions.
  • Some functions are too complicated or nuanced to make good game questions, so I initially left them out of the app data files. I am now adding these back in for completeness of this freestyle discovery feature.

Cross-Training

A true master is platform agnostic. You can toggle between Mac and PC shortcuts in the top left of the screen.

When training for the non-native environment, the app dynamically remaps the physical keys so that you will be learning their correct key positions for the functions on the other operating system.

To support this, the graphical keyboard will feature double labelling in this mode to clarify the cross-platform mapping.

Final Thoughts

Keyboard Warriors is designed to grow with you. The more you play, the more features, settings and training approaches become available.

You can treat it as a simple quiz game, a shortcut coach, a discovery tool, or all three at once. Experiment with the settings, try different display modes, and don't be afraid to follow your curiosity.

After all, that's how you ended up reading this far.
